Sound-event research after the BPM device study — 2026-11-14

Decision
Keep the installed v1.7 (12) model. The adapted research model rejects substantially
more synthetic contact noise, but it does not consistently improve full BPM results.
No new model, decoder, threshold or app update was installed during this research.
The work supplies a tested sound-event benchmark for later rhythm/murmur research;
it does not supply either disease classifier or establish 100% BPM accuracy.
No more participant recordings were requested or collected.

Why this matters
An extra or missed sound can invent or hide an irregular interval. BPM averages alone
cannot check that. EventProbe.swift exports S1/S2 sound candidates and masked spans
without a heart-rate regularity filter. Both original fragments and merged sound
episodes remain available. The merge only joins adjacent fragments of the same sound
within 50 ms, with a 250 ms maximum total span; it never smooths heart-to-heart timing.
Raw heart audio is preserved for future analysis of sounds between S1 and S2.

Experiments
All training uses the original 80 public CirCor linked-patient training groups and
16 validation groups. Neither personal audio, Watch values, ECG nor disease labels
train these candidates. Each model keeps the original eight-feature/five-state API.

Candidate 1: train a new model with irregular timing, clipping and contact-noise
augmentations. Despite better development sound-event scores, it worsened real
ECG comparisons; one returned BPM differed by 105.80. Rejected.

Candidate 2: start from the deployed weights; use the original network as a fixed
teacher while learning the new augmentations. Fixed 16 epochs, 100 steps, learning
rate 0.00015, 48 original/16 augmented examples per batch, temperature 2. Validation
selects epoch 15, including the original epoch 0 as an eligible choice. This retains
more original behavior and is the single candidate selected before the final test.
Eight native Core ML conversion examples differ by at most 0.000002384 in logits.

Candidate 3: a fixed equal-probability average of original and adapted models. No
weight search. It weakens the noise-rejection benefit and does not restore original
correct-reading availability on ECG. Not selected for the final patient test.

The already-inspected 22 personal WAVs and 108 EPHNOGRAM audio cases are regression/
development checks for these candidates, not new independent tests. Candidate 2
returns 1,392/1,516 warm updates on completed earlier chest trials (deployed v1.7:
1,395) and 792/844 on the final completed chest trials (v1.7: 791). All five saved
off-chest recordings still return no BPM. Watch disagreements remain; Watch is not
ECG ground truth. Stopped attempts, the cough annotation and the partial final test
remain in the local reports and are not discarded.

On the existing EPHNOGRAM recent-ECG comparison, candidate 2 returns 2,369 of 4,311
reference-eligible updates, versus 2,409 for v1.7. Mean absolute error on returned
values is 1.82 versus 1.80 BPM; maximum is 18.14 versus 15.41. The share of all
eligible updates with a returned value within 5 BPM falls from 52.01% to 50.99%.
This real-recording regression is one reason not to replace the installed model.
Automated ECG agreement remains a reference limitation; see ecg-validation-v17.txt.

Final patient test
24 previously unread linked-patient groups were reserved using metadata only,
excluding all 132 previously accessed CirCor groups. Eight groups per patient-level
murmur-label stratum balance selection; these labels are not model outputs. Candidate
weights, sources, metrics, cohort, runtime and both native executables were hashed
before any of these 24 WAVs or annotation files were downloaded. All 48 source
files match the published SHA256 checksums. No retraining or threshold changes
followed this test. This cohort is now consumed and must not be called unseen in
later development.

Seven fixed conditions per patient produce 168 cases: clean, irregular, contact,
irregular/contact, clipped, irregular/clipped and noise-only. Irregular timing changes
diastolic duration while retaining every S1/S2 waveform sample. It is synthetic timing
variation, not diagnosed AFib. Contact noise uses independent synthetic pulses at
6 dB signal-to-noise ratio; the generator is related to training augmentation, so
this is not proof of robustness to new kinds of real-world noise. Clipping is fixed
at the 90th absolute-amplitude quantile. Full methods are in model_holdout.py.

Public sound-state annotations are semi-supervised. Unknown spans remain unscored;
they are not silently treated as noise. The clean event reference covers 211.30
seconds across all 24 groups. Known synthetic noise-only audio covers 457.18 seconds.
Both models completed all 168 event cases and all 7,125 production updates.

Each reference definition has 5,277 post-warm-up updates, including 747 known

Updates overlap in time; seven conditions reuse each patient's recording. They
are not thousands of independent patients. An exploratory 8,000-draw patient-level
bootstrap gives a recent within-5 coverage change interval of -0.68 to +0.81
percentage points. It does not establish a reliable overall BPM improvement.

Consequences for the next phase
The separate sound-event path is useful research infrastructure. The current BPM
decoder expects fairly regular cycles: it often abstains on synthetic irregular
timing. Therefore a missing BPM or rejected cycle must never mean "no AFib," and
this decoder must not be the sole gate for a later rhythm study. Irregular positive
beats remain positive training examples and raw sound candidates remain available.
Synthetic irregularity cannot validate clinical AFib detection.

For murmurs, retain the original sound and analyze the frequency/time pattern,
including sound between beats. The five-state timing model does not classify
murmurs. A later classifier needs suitable public labels, patient-separated testing
and a distinct evaluation of transfer from a stethoscope to a phone microphone.
Improving BPM alone does not validate either disease task.

Reproduction and retained evidence
From ChestBPM, with the recorded local runtime:
  .venv-ml/bin/python ML/beat_model_v2.py
  .venv-ml/bin/python ML/beat_model_adapt.py
  .venv-ml/bin/python ML/beat_model_ensemble.py
Each refuses to overwrite an existing candidate checkpoint. Preserve existing exact
weights/protocols before using another output directory or conducting new research.
Native EventProbe.swift and ProductionReplay.swift consume audio-only requests;
reference labels never enter estimator input. model_holdout.py runs freeze,
download, prepare and evaluate in that order; freeze refuses reused source data.
summarize_model_holdout.py aggregates the completed test without changing results.
See ML/beat-v2-holdout.json for reserved groups and published source hashes.

Exact model checkpoints, conversion reports, native outputs, locked protocol,
runtime, source hashes, requests, full comparisons and aggregate.json remain under
ignored local ML/generated and diagnostic-logs/2026-11-14-event-model-research.
All 21 ECG/event/reference engineering tests passed. All original 15 evaluation
freeze inputs, including deployed weights and estimator sources, remain unchanged.
No new on-phone model has been installed or physically tested for these candidates.
